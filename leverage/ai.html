How I used AI

I used Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, as a collaborator on code, charts and drafts. I set the questions, reviewed every output, and made the final calls. This page shows where AI helped and how I checked its work.

Part 2

How I checked AI's work

  • I read every chart for ways it could mislead. I caught tick marks spaced unevenly, negative percentages that were hard to read, panels with different scales that looked comparable, legend dots whose sizes suggested a meaning they didn't have, labels covering data points, and a highlight box that cut through a bar.
  • I questioned numbers that didn't match. A card said Loyola had 1,237 doctors while the chart showed 708. The two counts were different measures, doctors in the group and doctors compared, so both labels were rewritten. When a filter returned fewer systems than I expected, I traced it to the wrong column: doctors compared instead of group size.
  • I asked for evidence beyond the sample. I asked whether the findings held for a second office-visit code, for doctors who couldn't be matched, and for all providers. When the second code matched the first almost exactly, I asked why instead of accepting it. The answer was that both plans price that code as a fixed multiple of the first.
  • I required sources for outside facts. The claim that three groups belong to one health system isn't in the price files, so I asked how we knew it. The page now cites the merger.
  • I checked that every claim covers the whole story. Statements written about office visits had to hold for the MRI as well, or say that they don't.
  • I reviewed the pages for privacy before publishing. No individual doctors, tax ID numbers or personal details appear.
